# Poob voice-bot latency plan for April 2026 (original report body)

**The single biggest win is architectural, not model swaps.** Fix the serial `route→search→wrap→TTS` chain first (speculative wrap during ytdl search) and you cut ~2s off music-play before touching a model. The Groq `llama-3.3-70b-versatile` 400s are a **confirmed Groq-side parser regression** (open since July 2025) — fix is to drop that model for `openai/gpt-oss-20b` and add a client-side `<function=...>` recovery regex. On-device STT on the 4090 beats cloud Deepgram on first-word for short commands by 200-350ms even before you touch the network. With the top three changes, realistic end-state is **~1.0-1.5s** to first word on music play (from 6.7s) and **~600-900ms** on casual reply (from 2-3s). Full-replacement S2S models (gpt-realtime, Moshi, Qwen3-Omni-30B) are **not the answer** under your zero-budget + 24GB + per-user-sink constraints — cascaded with streaming is still winning at P50 in 2026 per the arXiv 2603.05413 measurements, and no realtime API preserves per-speaker identity server-side anyway.

## Top 3 highest-leverage changes, rank-ordered

### 1. Speculative wrap + concurrent ytdl + streaming TTS (music-play path)
**Expected cut: ~2.0-2.5s off music play. Effort: low (1-2 days).** This is the dominant sink in your 6.7s budget and requires no new models. Your wrap is a function of the *query*, not the *result* — fan it out the instant routing resolves:

```
t=0      routing returns {"tool":"music_assistant","query":q}
t=0      asyncio.create_task(ytdl_search(q))        # 2-3s
t=0      asyncio.create_task(stream_wrap_llm(q))    # 0.5-1.5s, token-stream
t=0      asyncio.create_task(prewarm_ffmpeg_pool()) # kills 770ms cold
t≈300ms  flush first sentence boundary → streaming TTS
t≈500ms  first PCM frame plays (user hears Toob)
t≈2-3s   search result arrives → duck in music
t=fail   play recovery line ("couldn't find it, chief")
```

Combined with **sentence-boundary TTS streaming** (any of Orpheus-TTS, Cartesia Sonic-3 free tier, or ElevenLabs Flash v2.5 — all deliver first-chunk under 200ms), wrap audio starts ~500ms after end-of-speech instead of ~5s later. The correctness tradeoff — wrap references query not result — is a prompt-design problem, not an architecture problem. The "I couldn't find that" recovery path fires maybe 2% of calls. Pipecat's `pipecat-ai/nemotron-january-2026` reference bot (`bot_interleaved_streaming.py`) uses this exact pattern and clocks **465ms V2V TTFB on RTX 5090** — it is the single best public codebase to study.

Reference: `https://livekit.com/blog/sequential-pipeline-architecture-voice-agents`, `https://github.com/pipecat-ai/nemotron-january-2026`, arXiv `https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05413v1`.

### 2. Kill the Groq 400 retry + restructure the tool-call cascade
**Expected cut: 500ms off every tool call, plus eliminates the 200ms Cerebras 429 leg. Effort: low (2-4 hours).** The `llama-3.3-70b-versatile` failure is **not your schema** — it's a server-side Groq parser regression. The model emits Meta's native `<function=name>{...}</function>` wrapper and Groq's OpenAI-compat parser can't convert it, so the whole response 400s with `tool_use_failed`. **Confirmed unresolved since July 17, 2025** (Groq staff acknowledged at `https://community.groq.com/t/tool-use-fail-rate-increases-recently/438`, still reproducing April 2026 at `https://community.groq.com/t/why-do-llama-3-3-and-qwen3-fail-at-simple-file-editing/1261`, Feb 2026 GitHub repro at `https://github.com/sipeed/picoclaw/issues/748`).

Two-part fix:

**(a) Recover client-side immediately.** Parse `failed_generation` with regex `<function=([a-zA-Z0-9_-]+)\s*(\{.*?\})\s*</function>` on any 400 `code: "tool_use_failed"` — recovers ~99% of routings with zero retry tax. Five minutes of code.

**(b) Rebuild the cascade.** New order: `Groq openai/gpt-oss-20b` → `Self-hosted Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 (vLLM, 4090)` → `OpenRouter mistralai/mistral-small-3.2-24b-instruct:free` → `NVIDIA NIM qwen3-next-80b` (keeper). **`openai/gpt-oss-20b` uses OpenAI Harmony format natively** and does not exhibit the regression; 1000+ t/s on Groq, tool calls not charged (promo), 1000 req/day free, 8K TPM. Self-hosted Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 on your 4090 with `vllm serve --enable-auto-tool-choice --tool-call-parser hermes` gives 40-150ms TTFT, BFCL-v3 71%, zero rate limits, 256K context. Drop llama-3.3-70b-versatile and the Cerebras 235B leg entirely.

Also on your three-tool schema: confirm every object has `additionalProperties: false`, no `$ref`/`oneOf`/`anyOf` (Groq's parser is stricter than OpenAI's), boolean fields enumerated explicitly in the description, `temperature: 0.2-0.3`, `tool_choice: "auto"`, no `parallel_tool_calls`. `https://console.groq.com/docs/model/openai/gpt-oss-20b` · `https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507`.

### 3. Move STT on-device (Nemotron-Speech-Streaming-0.6B or Parakeet-TDT v3 on 4090)
**Expected cut: 200-350ms off first-word latency; eliminates 100-200ms WAN. Effort: medium (1-2 days).** Your 4090 running `nvidia/nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b` hits **80ms-chunk TTFT natively streaming** via cache-aware FastConformer-RNNT (WER 7.16% at 1120ms chunk, 8.53% at 80ms chunk) vs Deepgram Nova-3's 400-500ms end-to-end including WAN. Nova-3 is still the **cloud** latency leader in independent Coval benchmarks, but any sub-200ms on-device inference trivially beats it once you add WAN.

Keyterm biasing replacement: NeMo 2.5.0+ ships `rnnt_decoding.beam.boosting_tree.key_phrases_file` for TDT/RNN-T/CTC — drop `play, skip, stop, pause, volume, shuffle` in that file, done (`https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/latest/nemotoolkit/asr/asr_customization/word_boosting.html`, merged PR #14277).

Alternative if you want pre-built hot-word command spotting for a fixed vocabulary: **sherpa-onnx KWS** (`sherpa-onnx-kws-zipformer-zh-en-3M-2025-12-20`) is literally designed for this — 80ms chunk streaming, built-in hot-word list with per-word boost scores, Apache 2.0, runs CPU or 4090. If you stay cloud, the 2026 incumbent to switch Nova-3 → **Deepgram Flux** (Oct 2025, `flux-general-en`) — Coval measures it as the fastest cloud STT with ~450ms P50 improvement over Nova-3, and keeps the same keyterm API. `https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b` · `https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/kws/pretrained_models/index.html` · `https://deepgram.com/learn/introducing-flux-conversational-speech-recognition`.

## Targeted answers to your seven questions

### Q1 — End-to-end realtime models
**Verdict: don't replace the triple. Use S2S selectively.** No realtime API (gpt-realtime, Gemini Live, Grok Voice, Nova 2 Sonic) preserves **per-speaker identity server-side** — all treat input as single-stream. You'd mix per-user Discord Opus client-side, tag speakers with name markers, and open one session per active speaker. April 2026 TTFA rankings (Artificial Analysis Big Bench Audio): **Gemini 2.5 Flash Native Audio Dialog 0.63s** (fastest + only real free tier) / Grok Voice 0.78s / Qwen3.5-Omni-Flash 0.79s / gpt-realtime-mini 0.81s / gpt-realtime 0.98s / Nova 2 Sonic 1.14s. OpenAI pricing ($0.06/$0.24 per minute in/out) violates your zero-budget constraint. **Best free-tier cloud path: Gemini 2.5 Flash Native Audio Dialog** via Google AI Studio (Gemini 3.1 Flash Live regressed async function calling per `https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/live-api/tools`, Mar 2026 — stick with 2.5). **Best self-host path: Kyutai Unmute** (`https://github.com/kyutai-labs/unmute`) wrapping Kyutai STT 1B + Kyutai TTS 1.6B around a local tool-calling LLM — Apache/CC-BY, 200-350ms TTFA end-to-end, leaves 18+ GB free on the 4090 for the LLM. Collapsing 4 stages into one call is only worthwhile on the **casual-reply path** (Gemini Live with function calling, score 71.5% on ComplexFuncBench Audio vs gpt-realtime's 30.5% on MultiChallenge — Gemini is materially stronger at audio-mode tool calling); keep the cascade for music since S2S models can't do yt-dlp anyway.

### Q2 — Streaming TTS
**Winner under your constraints: Orpheus-TTS 3B self-hosted on 4090 via vLLM + SNAC.** Apache 2.0, ~160ms first chunk on 4090 (taresh18 streaming fork), inline `<angry>` / `<laugh>` / `<sigh>` / `<chuckle>` tags that are a perfect match for your warlord Toob persona and survive your FFmpeg chain intact. Repo `https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS`, streaming fork `https://github.com/taresh18/orpheus-streaming`. **Hedge: Cartesia Sonic-3** (90ms TTFA, native `[laugh]`/`[sigh]` tags, 10K chars/month free tier — enough for a personal bot, `https://docs.cartesia.ai/`). **Watch: Qwen3-TTS 1.7B** (`https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS`, Apache 2.0, ~97ms first-packet claimed, VoiceDesign prompt interface for warlord voice) — promising but less battle-tested than Orpheus. CosyVoice 2 (`https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice`) third-best open option at ~150ms first-packet with TensorRT-LLM. **Don't chase: StyleTTS 3 not released; Piper archived Oct 2025; Fish S1 non-commercial license; XTTS v2 non-commercial; Chatterbox first-chunk 472ms too slow; ElevenLabs v3 (Mar 2026 GA) explicitly NOT real-time — only Flash v2.5 streams.** Exploit 2026-specific tooling: persistent vLLM server for KV-cache reuse, input-text streaming from LLM to TTS (shaves 100ms), pre-warmed CUDA context (sends tiny primer on startup — saves 100-300ms on true first request).

### Q3 — Wake-word replacement
**Use `livekit-wakeword` v0.1.0 (Feb 2026, Apache 2.0).** Backward compatible with your current `hey_poob.onnx` feature front-end (Google speech embedding + oww 96-dim → 16×96 matrix) but replaces openwakeword's flat DNN head with **1D Conv + Multi-Head Self-Attention**. Benchmark vs openwakeword on 25h mixed audio: FPPH 8.50 → **0.08** (100× fewer false positives), recall 68.6% → **86.1%**, AUT 0.072 → 0.0012. Directly fixes both your symptoms: conv-attention has inductive bias for temporal order (fixes noisy-game false positives from phonemes in wrong order) + aggressive music-augmentation recipe with -10 to +15 dB SNR (fixes missed wakes under music duck). `https://github.com/livekit/livekit-wakeword` · `https://livekit.com/blog/livekit-wakeword`. Training pipeline: piper-sample-generator v3.1.0 for 10K synthetic positives, 250 adversarial negatives auto-generated, mix in FMA commercial music at -10 to +15 dB SNR, MIT impulse-response reverb, **2-5 hours of your own Discord+gameplay audio** as custom background dataset (this is the killer step), then `uv run livekit-wakeword run hey_poob.yaml`. Target FPPH <0.2, recall ≥90%. Share one ONNX session across Discord users with per-user rolling buffers — fixes the Silero per-user-init problem architecturally. Backup: Picovoice Porcupine v3 (2026) if you can accept the 3-user commercial free tier; best published noise-robustness but closed-weight. **Skip: microWakeWord (no desktop API), WavLM-base (94M params too heavy), Whisper-based pseudo-wake (100+ms CPU), EfficientWord-Net (weak noise benchmark), Mycroft Howl/Precise (abandoned).**

### Q4 — STT alternatives
Covered in Top 3 #3. Summary: **on-device wins decisively** for short commands. Latency math for 2s "play lofi hip hop": cloud Deepgram = 300ms server + 100-200ms WAN = 400-500ms first word; Moonshine-tiny on 4090 = **15-40ms** forward pass; Nemotron-Speech-Streaming 80ms chunk TTFT native streaming. **Top 3 ranked: (1) NVIDIA Nemotron-Speech-Streaming-0.6B** native streaming, NeMo word-boosting, `https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-speech-streaming-en-0.6b`. **(2) NVIDIA Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3** offline ~30-80ms for 3s clip on 4090, NeMo 2.5.0 boosting_tree, `https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3`. **(3) sherpa-onnx Zipformer KWS** Apache 2.0 built-in hot-words + streaming, `https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/kws/pretrained_models/index.html`. Cloud fallback if you keep Deepgram: **switch Nova-3 → Flux** (keeps keyterm API, ~450ms faster P50). **Skip: Moonshine** (fastest on paper but no keyterm API — would need n-gram LM rescoring); **Groq whisper-large-v3-turbo is segmented batch, not streaming** despite the branding — 80ms per segment but "wait for segment" + WAN dominates.

### Q5 — Tool-calling LLMs
Covered in Top 3 #2. Comparison targets for your 3-tool schema on 4090: **Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507** (8GB FP16, 40-150ms TTFT, BFCL-v3 71%, 256K context, Hermes tokens, `vllm serve --tool-call-parser hermes`) is the latency/reliability leader among self-host options. **Llama-xLAM-2-8b-fc-r** (`https://huggingface.co/Salesforce/Llama-xLAM-2-8b-fc-r`) if routing quality matters more — Salesforce reports beats GPT-4o + Claude 3.5 on BFCL-v3 agentic. **Mistral Small 3.2 24B** free on OpenRouter (`mistralai/mistral-small-3.2-24b-instruct:free`) best quality hosted fallback. **Phi-4-mini has documented function-name hallucination** on verbose schemas — reject for passive-chat context. **Phi-5, xLAM-v3, Llama 4.x smaller-than-Scout don't exist April 2026.** Qwen3 and Hermes-4-14B with dedicated `<tool_call>` tokens are structurally harder to confuse from passive context than JSON-in-prose models.

### Q6 — Architectural patterns
Covered in Top 3 #1. The serial wrap-waits-on-search is the single costliest mistake in your current architecture. **Public codebases worth studying:** (a) **`pipecat-ai/nemotron-january-2026`** — `bot_interleaved_streaming.py` shows buffered-LLM + adaptive-TTS + SmartTurn + 100% KV cache reuse → 465ms V2V TTFB on RTX 5090, directly transferable patterns. (b) **`GabrielAgrela/Discord-Brain-Rot`** — same author as your DAVE patch, actively maintained through 2026, Pycord-based, has slash-soundboard + live voice keyword detection + buffered AI commentary with memory/cooldown, LLM profiles with primary+fallback, Dockerized. (c) **`C4se-K/Real-Time-Transcription-Discord-Bot`** for the threaded architecture intercepting raw Opus packets before Pycord decodes them — useful if you want to pipe directly to a realtime WebSocket without round-tripping through Pycord's sink. **No active public Discord bot uses OpenAI Realtime or Gemini Live with Pycord sinks — you're in greenfield.** Writing a Pipecat `DiscordTransport` (bridging Pycord `VoiceClient`/sinks to Pipecat's `BaseInputTransport`/`BaseOutputTransport`) is high-effort but would unlock the entire Pipecat plugin ecosystem. Skip for now; revisit after top 3 land. FFmpeg optimization: pre-warmed FFmpeg process pool (2-4 pre-initialized chains, hand out per-play, tear down cold in background) kills your 770ms cold start; move pitch+tempo+resample to **torchaudio on the 4090** (single kernel, <10ms for short sounds) and leave FFmpeg only for the decorative `vibrato,bass,aecho` filters.

### Q7 — Groq 400 root cause
Covered in Top 3 #2. TL;DR: **Groq's post-generation parser cannot handle Meta's native `<function=name>{...}</function>` wrapper**, which Llama-3.3-70B-Versatile started emitting ~mid-2025. Groq acknowledged July 2025, unresolved April 2026. Not your schema. Mitigations (ranked): (1) client-side regex recovery from `failed_generation`, (2) switch Groq primary to `openai/gpt-oss-20b` (Harmony format, not affected), (3) add `response_format: {type: "json_object"}`, (4) temperature 0.1 + one-shot example. Thread: `https://community.groq.com/t/tool-use-fail-rate-increases-recently/438`. LibreChat #12178 is a **separate** Groq bug on schema boolean-type coercion — audit your `music_assistant`/`deal_assistant` params for boolean fields and make sure types are exact.

To utilize a Realtime API, the system must either mix the audio locally and send a single stream, or manage parallel connections. Mixing the audio locally destroys speaker identity, rendering the AI unable to distinguish between primary users giving commands and background users conversing, effectively paralyzing the conversational context. Conversely, opening a separate WebSocket or WebRTC connection for every user in the channel completely isolates the conversational context. The AI agent handling one user has no internal knowledge of what another user just said, breaking the shared communal experience that defines a voice bot in a group setting.While some APIs offer metadata tagging or parallel stream support in beta, the underlying attention mechanisms of these models are overwhelmingly trained on dyadic (one-on-one) interactions. Attempting to force multi-user context through a single cloud multimodal session results in severe hallucination regarding speaker attribution and intent resolution.1.2 The Diarization and Wake-Gating ParadoxThe operational constraints require local wake-gating to control API costs and prevent the AI from responding to ambient chatter or background game audio. Realtime APIs are designed as stateful, continuous streaming sessions where the model maintains a rolling buffer of acoustic context. If the audio stream is gated locally and only opened when the wake word is detected, the Realtime API loses the preceding conversational context and the acoustic pre-roll necessary to maintain a natural cadence.Furthermore, continuously opening and closing WebSockets or WebRTC peer connections upon wake-word detection introduces Transport Layer Security (TLS) handshake overhead and Interactive Connectivity Establishment (ICE) negotiation delays. This network initialization overhead effectively neutralizes the latency benefits of the Realtime API, rendering it slower than a highly optimized, persistent serialized pipeline. While cloud-based diarization models exist—such as the gpt-4o-transcribe-diarize endpoints—they introduce significant inter-chunk latency and routinely struggle to separate overlapping speakers in noisy environments, making them unsuitable for real-time game audio contexts.1.3 The Orchestrated Edge-Hybrid ImperativeGiven the strict requirement to ingest per-user audio streams with preserved identity, run local wake-gating, and operate without a commercial-scale inference budget, migrating to a unified cloud Realtime API is strongly counter-indicated. When a user speaks, their isolated audio stream is passed through their dedicated inference thread. The resulting text is appended to the master conversation context array, tagged with the Discord User ID. This guarantees absolute accuracy in speaker identification with zero diarization overhead, providing a massive functional advantage over cloud APIs while maintaining data sovereignty and eliminating ongoing inference costs.2. Diagnosing and Resolving LLM Tool-Calling FailuresThe current production pipeline relies on the Groq-hosted llama-3.3-70b-versatile model for intent routing and tool calling. Unlike the OpenAI or Anthropic APIs, which frequently implement lenient type coercion at the ingestion layer—automatically converting a string value like "true" to a boolean true—the Groq API enforces strict schema validation that must match the exact parameter types defined in the provided JSON schema. If the tool schema defines a parameter as a boolean or an integer, and the model outputs a string representation, the API rejects the request entirely, resulting in a 400 Bad Request error and an aborted operation. Llama 3.3, depending on the exact system prompt, is prone to wrapping boolean and integer values in quotation marks when generating JSON objects.The second, more pervasive failure mode involves a syntax hallucination directly linked to the instruction-tuning data used by Meta for the Llama 3.3 models. Acoustic Wake-Word Resilience in High-Noise EnvironmentsThe openwakeword implementation utilizing a custom hey_poob.onnx model currently exhibits unacceptable behavior, firing false positives from background game audio and missing clean wake words when music plays underneath the speaker. In environments with heavy, non-stationary background noise—such as gunfire, explosions, or complex game soundtracks—the spectral energy overlaps heavily with human speech formants. The spectrogram effectively "blurs," causing small-footprint models to fail on their classification boundaries.Furthermore, depending on the specific Python environment (specifically Python 3.12 and above), openwakeword suffers from significant upstream dependency degradation, making it increasingly difficult to maintain in a modern containerized stack. This pre-training task forces the model to learn robust phonetic representations that inherently separate human speech patterns from background acoustic noise. By passing the incoming audio chunk through a HuBERT-tiny layer, the system obtains a dense feature vector that represents the phonetic content of the audio, highly independent of the overlapping game audio.The inference of a tiny SSL model using the ONNX Runtime on a modern CPU takes approximately 15 to 25 milliseconds. The subsequent linear classifier execution takes less than 1 millisecond. This easily fits within the 50ms budget while providing an exponential increase in robustness compared to MFCC-based models.4.3 Custom Training Pipeline and Echo CancellationTo retain the custom "Hey Poob" wake word, a new training pipeline must be established. Developers must collect 50 to 100 clean, diverse samples of the wake word spoken by various users. The audio is passed through the frozen HuBERT-tiny model to extract embeddings. Cloud-based tool-calling introduces network jitter and stringent rate limits that shatter real-time immersion. Local SLMs are the definitive solution for routing in 2026.Llama 4 Scout (17B) Deployment: While an exceptional model, deploying a 17B active-parameter MoE model locally is counter-productive. It is too large to reside comfortably alongside a 2GB TTS and a 2GB STT model in a single 24GB VRAM buffer without extreme quantization that severely degrades JSON syntax adherence and tool-calling accuracy.LLM-based Wake-Word Verification: Do not attempt to stream audio continuously to a small LLM to detect intent without a primary acoustic gate. The CPU overhead and constant VRAM state thrashing will destabilize the Pycord audioop mixing layer. The architecture must retain dedicated, microscopic acoustic models for wake detection.Fast Cloud TTS (Kokoro v2 API, ElevenLabs): Even if a cloud TTS is infinitely fast, waiting for sentence boundaries from the LLM imposes a hard physical limit of ~500-800ms latency due to linguistic chunking. Do not evaluate TTS models that cannot perform sub-sentence, token-interleaved streaming directly on the edge hardware.
